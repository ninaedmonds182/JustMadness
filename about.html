About JustMadness

Why JustMadness Exists ⭐

How It All Started

It started with one birthday.

I'm a mum.

When my daughter had her first proper birthday party, we booked a soft play centre. It was easy. It was popular. It was exactly what everyone seemed to do.

Two hours later it was over.

The children had run everywhere, climbed everything and eaten the cake.

Driving home, I remember thinking...

"I'm not even sure she knew it was her party."

Nothing had gone wrong. It just could have been anybody's birthday.

That was the moment I realised I wasn't looking for a party. I was looking for a story.

So I Stopped Looking

I searched everywhere for something different. Most parties were either expensive entertainment packages where someone else did everything... or printable treasure hunts that felt more like homework than adventure.

Neither was what I imagined.

I wanted children to feel like they'd stepped inside a film. I wanted them solving problems, laughing together, making ridiculous decisions and talking about what happened all the way home.

So I started building it myself.

The Children Became My Test Team

Every time I tested something with children, I learned something. Not because I asked for feedback... but because children are wonderfully honest.

If something was boring, they wandered off. If something was confusing, they ignored it. If something was exciting, they forgot I was even there.

Those were the moments I kept. Everything else disappeared.

What JustMadness Became

Today it isn't just a birthday party. It's a mission.

Children use technology to enter the story. Then they spend the next couple of hours doing everything except staring at a screen.

Searching. Running. Solving. Baking. Decorating. Working together.
Laughing at things that go completely wrong.
Cheering for the dog who somehow gets it right.

By the end, they haven't just completed a set of games. They've been part of a story they'll still be talking about next week.

Why I Still Make Them

Because I still believe the best parties aren't the ones with the biggest budget.

They're the ones where, weeks later, somebody says...

"Do you remember when Professor Baker froze the recipe?"

Or...

"Do you remember when we all had dog names?"

That's the sort of party I wanted for my daughter.

That's still the sort of party I hope every child gets to have. And now, finally, it exists.
